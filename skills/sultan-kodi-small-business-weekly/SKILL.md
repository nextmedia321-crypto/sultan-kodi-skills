# Small business weekly brief

## Inputs
Use whatever is available: accounting data, payment processor, CRM, calendar, email, or numbers the user pastes. If nothing is connected, ask for the few numbers needed. Never invent figures; mark gaps as "not provided".

## Brief structure (one page, the user's language)
1. **Headline:** one sentence on how the week went versus last week.
2. **Money:** cash in, cash out, balance or runway if known, and what is due in the next 14 days.
3. **Sales:** revenue this week versus last week and versus the same week last month; top products or services.
4. **Leads and pipeline:** new leads, follow-ups waiting, deals at risk.
5. **Open invoices:** who owes what and how late; who needs a reminder.
6. **Bills and costs:** upcoming payments and anything unusual.
7. **Risks:** the one or two things that could hurt next week.
8. **Three actions for the week:** concrete, ordered by value, each with who and when.

Keep it scannable. State the data date and which sources were used. Do not send messages or make payments; draft them for the owner to approve.

**Disclaimer:** information only, not accounting, tax or legal advice.

## Output template
One page in this order: `Headline | Money (in, out, due in 14 days) | Sales (vs last week) | Leads | Open invoices (who, how late) | Costs | Risks | Three actions (who, when)`. Each number carries its source and date; missing data is written "not provided".

## Example
Headline: "Sales up 8% on last week, but two invoices (4,200 and 1,900) are 20+ days late." Action 1: "Send reminders to both clients today (owner)."
Draft messages are shown for approval, never sent.

## Edge cases
- Only partial data: produce the sections you can and list what is missing at the top.
- Contradicting sources: show both numbers and say which one you used.
- Never move money, send emails or change records.
